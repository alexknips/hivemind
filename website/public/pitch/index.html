01

The problem / what breaks

Teams don't lose code.
They lose the reasons.

Six months after a decision the code is still there, but the why is gone — a Slack thread nobody can find, a doc that got overwritten, the head of someone who left. So the team has the same argument again, or quietly reverses a call that was right for reasons they no longer remember.

02

Why now / the shift

The faster they go,
the less we can see.

That agents make us faster is settled. The cost rides along with the speed — more decisions, and more messages, agent to agent and human to agent, than any team can read. Right when the work needs more governance and observability, it gets less.

Hundreds of choices a day, buried in traffic nobody has time to open — moving faster than anyone can account for.

03

The status quo / why it fails

Wikis are documents
pretending to be records.

A wiki page is whatever the last person typed. It can't tell you a decision is disputed, can't tell you it went stale because the assumption under it turned out false, can't prove who decided what.

04

The product / what it is

HiveMind is
the record.

It captures a decision the moment it's made — as structured data, not prose — in an append-only ledger that can't be quietly edited later. Every decision carries the options that lost, the evidence it stood on, and the assumptions it bet on.

05

How it works / the mechanism

Status you
can't fake.

  • DerivedStatus falls out of the graph, not a field someone sets.
  • ContestedPush back and both sides are kept — disagreement is a state, not a comment.
  • StaleDisprove an assumption and every decision leaning on it flags itself.
  • SignedEvery entry attributed. Supersession keeps the old version too.
06

The wedge / how it gets in

The agents capture
it themselves.

HiveMind speaks MCP, so Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor write their decisions down as they work — no extra step, no context switch, every entry signed. Humans come in too: import their decisions, and pull in the documents behind them as evidence — your existing docs get referenced, not replaced.

The thing making the problem worse becomes the thing that fixes it.

07

Defensibility / the moat

Hard to copy,
on purpose.

Immutability, derived status, staleness, required provenance — these are structural. A general-purpose workspace optimizes for flexibility; provenance needs constraint. You can't bolt one onto the other.

08

Origin / why this is real

I asked why.
They sent the whole transcript.

We were migrating a big, highly entangled Scala monolith after a model change — dozens of small, load-bearing decisions a week. I'd ask an engineer why they'd done something, and they'd hand over the entire Claude Code session. Thousands of lines: everything that happened, and nothing about why.

Then I realized I was no better. On my own hard calls, I couldn't say when I'd changed my mind, or what changed it. A transcript isn't reasoning — and that gap is the product: decision memory, not another log.
